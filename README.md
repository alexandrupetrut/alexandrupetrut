## Paw Heist 🐾

**Paw Heist** is a heartfelt cozy 3D stealth pet-heist adventure where mischievous pets pull off tiny household heists to help a scared rescue puppy feel safe, loved, and at home.

> **Tiny crimes. Big hearts.**
> Snatch snacks. Dodge guards. Escape cute.

The game is being designed around:

* **Short replayable heists** — steal snacks, avoid humans, manage evidence, and escape with the goods.
* **Heartfelt story progression** — every tiny crime helps the rescue puppy trust the world a little more.
* **Exploration and investigation** — inspect rooms, discover clues, unlock routes, and improve the puppy’s den.
* **Evidence trails** — crumbs, pawprints, fur, squeaky toys, knocked objects, and Roomba chaos.
* **Shareable Case Files** — every run ends with a funny crime report.
* **Pet abilities** — different pets solve the same heist in different ways.
* **Platform-aware builds** — designed with iOS, Android, and Steam in mind from the beginning.

Paw Heist is developed under **A&A PlayForge**, an indie game label by **Aura and Alex Popa**.

## Game dev direction

I’m especially interested in the overlap between software architecture, AI, and game development:

* **Gameplay systems** — player abilities, progression, inventory, missions, stealth loops, pets/companions, evidence systems, and reusable gameplay modules.
* **Story-driven systems** — campaign progression, exploration, environmental storytelling, emotional payoff, mission context, and cinematic beats.
* **AI-assisted development** — concept generation, asset pipeline support, dialogue/NPC tooling, procedural content ideas, and design iteration.
* **3D production workflow** — Unity-first development, Unreal Engine exploration, Blender-based asset workflows, animation, cameras, and cinematics.
* **Platform-aware delivery** — designing small-team projects with iOS, Android, and Steam in mind from the beginning.
* **Live product thinking** — analytics, telemetry, feature flags, safe monetisation, content updates, and operational visibility.

## Build philosophy

* Prefer small, testable slices over giant rewrites.
* Design systems that are easy to observe, operate, and evolve.
* Keep architecture practical: enough structure to scale, not enough to suffocate.
* Use AI as a force multiplier, not as a replacement for product judgment.
* Build games like products: clear hook, tight feedback loop, telemetry, iteration, and emotional payoff.
